##### Pair: GDPR ↔ NIS2

**GDPR article (verbatim, Art. 32(1)(d) + Art. 35(11)):**
> "[The controller and processor shall implement appropriate technical and organisational measures, such as … ] (d) a process for regularly testing, assessing and evaluating the effectiveness of technical and organisational measures for ensuring the security of the processing." — Art. 32(1)(d) GDPR
> "[The controller shall carry out a review to assess whether the processing operations are performed in accordance with the data protection impact assessment] at least when there is a change of the risk represented by processing operations." — Art. 35(11) GDPR

**NIS2 article (verbatim, Art. 21(2)(f)):**
> "[Member States shall ensure that essential and important entities take appropriate and proportionate technical, operational and organisational measures to … ] (f) policies and procedures to assess the effectiveness of cybersecurity risk-management measures." — Art. 21(2)(f) NIS 2 (per SR-NIS2-037/038)

**Scope comparison:**
- GDPR scope: regular testing of security-measure effectiveness + DPIA review on risk change.
- NIS 2 scope: effectiveness-assessment policy + scheduled procedures operationalising effectiveness measurement.
- Scope overlap: **Y (when the controller-NSIE adopts testing programmes)**.

**Trigger comparison:**
- GDPR trigger: effectiveness-evaluation + DPIA review on risk change.
- NIS 2 trigger: effectiveness-assessment policy.
- Trigger alignment: **PARTIAL** (different scopes — security measures vs. risk-management measures).

**Threshold/timeline comparison:**
- GDPR: `regularly testing` (DPO-discretionary cadence) + `at least when there is a change of the risk` (Art. 35(11)).
- NIS 2: no quantitative cadence (entity-discretionary under Recital 56).
- Tension: **NONE**.

**Recipient comparison:**
- GDPR: data subject, DPA.
- NIS 2: management body, competent authority.

**Content template comparison:**
- GDPR: not codified for content.
- NIS 2: not codified for content.

**Classified relationship (from CRDA):** COMPLEMENTARY
**Verified relationship (after OJ-text analysis):** **SAME — COMPLEMENTARY**
**Reasoning:** GDPR testing is effectiveness-evaluation; NIS 2 testing is effectiveness-assessment with feedback into risk-management cycle. The two regimes overlap in objective (effectiveness) but with different scopes (security measures vs. risk-management measures).

**Scope-disjoint test:** Does this pair actually overlap on a single event at a single party? **Y (when the controller-NSIE adopts testing programmes)**. No reclassification required.

**Downstream implication (Phase 1C Doc 07):** Phase 2 obligation derivation must produce a layered stack: GDPR Art. 32(1)(d) effectiveness-evaluation + GDPR Art. 35(11) DPIA review + NIS 2 Art. 21(2)(f) effectiveness-assessment.

##### Art. 5(1)(f) — Integrity and confidentiality

**Clause: GDPR-CL06** | type: principle | obligatedParty: CONTROLLER | obligationType: CONTINUOUS

**Berry anchor:** §5.1 (`appropriate`), §5.4.7 (`or` coordination).

**Instance 1 — VAG / S3 / `appropriate security`, `appropriate technical or organisational measures`**

`appropriate` is the recurring Berry-flagged term. `appropriate technical or organisational measures` uses OR whose inclusivity is unspecified. Art. 32 (security of processing) anchors `appropriate` to a risk-based test but does not resolve the OR.

**Variant readings:**
| # | Reading | Disambiguation source |
|---|---|---|
| R1 | `or` is inclusive: at least one of technical OR organisational measures required. | Strict grammatical reading. |
| R2 | `or` is inclusive: both required in combination (despite `or`). | Industry reading; ISO 27001 control set interpretation. |
| R3 | `or` is exclusive: the controller chooses which category. | Loose reading. |

R1 vs R2 yield materially distinct compliance obligations (R1 permits policy-only compliance for low-tech processing; R2 requires both).

**Instance 2 — COORD / S3 / `unauthorised or unlawful processing`, `accidental loss, destruction or damage`**

Two OR coordinations enumerating breach types. The 5-fold enumeration (unauthorised / unlawful / loss / destruction / damage) is similar to Art. 4(12) `personal data breach` but not identical (Art. 4(12) adds `alteration`, `disclosure of`, `access to`).

| # | Reading | Disambiguation source |
|---|---|---|
| R1 | Any of the 5 events triggers `appropriate security` obligations. | Strict text. |

R1 vs R2 yield materially distinct compliance obligations on breach response.

| `penetration testing` | Black-box vs. grey-box vs. white-box | §3.3.1 (`penetration`) |

Each row is S2 on its own (the modality is recognised; the specific implementation is open). The aggregate is the open-list pattern at S3.

**Definitional anchor:** DORA Art. 3(2) → NIS 2 Art. 6(1) `network and information system`; DORA-D17 `TLPT` (for the penetration-testing sub-modality when elevated to TLPT, see CL26-*).
**Proportionality-status:** Art. 4 applies.

#### 6.1 Art. 25(1) — 12-modality open list (POLY/S3)

Art. 25(1) enumerates 12 specific test modalities under the `such as …` open-list signal. The structural pattern is **identical to CRA Annex I Part I §2(d)** (`authentication, identity or access management systems` under `including but not limited to`) and to the CRA Annex I enumeration generally.

- **Berry anchor:** §5.1 `such as` open-list marker; same pattern as CRA-C05 (Annex I Part I §2(d)).
- **Structural implication:** the 12 modalities are illustrative, not exhaustive. Financial entities may add (e.g. red team, social engineering, fuzz testing) but should not omit any of the 12 without justification.
- **Cross-regulation consistency:** CRA, NIS 2 (Art. 21(2)(e) `secure system development and maintenance` includes similar open enumerations), and DORA all converge on the open-list pattern. This is a **regulatory-drafting convention**, not an artefact of any single regulation.

#### 2.3 AIA-C03 — Art. 9(6) Testing for risk management

**Source locus:** `High-risk AI systems shall be tested for the purpose of identifying the most appropriate and targeted risk management measures. Testing shall ensure that high-risk AI systems perform consistently for their intended purpose`.

* **VAG — S2** — `most appropriate`, `targeted`, `consistently`.
* **POLY — S2** — `testing` — unit testing vs. integration testing vs. system testing vs. acceptance testing vs. red-teaming. The clause does not signal which.
* **COORD — S2** — `most appropriate AND targeted` (AND).

**Berry anchor:** §5.1 (`most appropriate`, `consistently`), §3.3.1 (`testing`).

#### 2.4 AIA-C04 — Art. 9(7) Real-world testing

**Source locus:** `Testing procedures may include testing in real-world conditions in accordance with Article 60`.

* **POLY — S2** — `testing in real-world conditions` — distinct from lab/sandbox testing; scope defined partly by cross-reference to Art. 60 but the boundary (pilot deployment? A/B test? live inference?) is left to Art. 60 detailed rules.

**Berry anchor:** §3.3.1 (`real-world conditions`).
