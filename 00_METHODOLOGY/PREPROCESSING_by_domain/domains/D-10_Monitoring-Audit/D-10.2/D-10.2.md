#### Cross-recipient map

D-10.2 generates a layered recipient set on the **audit-records evidence trail**. The recipients are at distinct regulatory channels on distinct record types but the channels do not generate a notification cascade of D-10.2 itself — they are **audit-records channels** that feed distinct regulatory artefacts on the same record object (or different record objects in scope-disjoint pairs):

- **GDPR** — data subject + supervisory authority (DPA) on the Art. 30(3) compliance records + Art. 5(2) accountability evidence + Art. 31 SA cooperation (records `made available to the supervisory authority on request`).

#### Scope-overlap principle (operational)

The high-level SO (`SO-D-10.2.HL`) engages on a given party **if and only if ≥2 applicable regulations have ≥1 Y or Conditional-Y-scope-overlap pair on the same party** (e.g. a DORA financial entity that is also a controller with CRA-conformant products deploying high-risk AI — a vertically-integrated financial-controller-manufacturer-AI-provider with GDPR + CRA + DORA + AI Act applicability on the layered 4-audit-records architecture, with the GDPR ↔ CRA pair substantively scope-disjoint by record-type distinctness but the 4-reg stack engages on the joint-condition operator via the Conditional-Y pairs involving DORA and/or AI Act). The four audit-logging mandates are stacked, not collapsed: compliance records (GDPR) + product documentation (CRA) + CIA+A information security policy (DORA) + AI-system event logs (AI Act). The high-level SO surfaces the common audit-logging duty but does **NOT** collapse the per-reg anchor, scope dimension, trigger, threshold, recipient, or content template, **and does NOT need to resolve any emergent tension** because no OJ conflict exists at D-10.2. **NIS 2 does not emit a sub-SO** — fully out-of-scope per CRDA T3-vs-text gap.

##### Art. 5(1)(c) — Data minimisation

**Clause: GDPR-CL03** | type: principle | obligatedParty: CONTROLLER | obligationType: CONTINUOUS

> 1. Processing shall be lawful only if and to the extent that at least one of the following applies:

#### 6.2 Art. 5(2) — the 4-verb coordination as DORA's governance signature

Art. 5(2) uses the **4-verb coordination `define, approve, oversee and be responsible for`** to describe the management body's role. This is the **longest management-body coordination pattern in DORA** and the operational analog of:

- **DORA-D01 `build, assure and review`** (3-verb, definition of digital operational resilience; pilot file §2.1)
- **CRA Art. 13(1) `designed, developed and produced`** (3-verb, manufacturer duty; CRA-CL14)
- **GDPR Art. 5(1)(a)–(f) principles** (6 principles, no verb coordination; analogous to a list of duties)

The 4-verb coordination is **structurally distinct** from the 3-verb patterns in DORA-D01 and CRA-CL14 because it adds the `be responsible for` element — the **non-delegable accountability** anchor. The first three verbs (`define, approve, oversee`) describe **processes**; the fourth (`be responsible for`) describes the **outcome liability**.

- **Berry anchor:** §5.4.7 (4-way coordination); §5.5 (verb parallelism); §3.3.1 (`responsible for`).
- **Structural implication:** the 4-verb pattern is the **signature DORA governance clause**. It propagates to Art. 6(1) (framework chapeau — `sound, comprehensive and well-documented` is the framework's qualities, mirroring the management body's four duties), to Art. 5(2)(a) (`ultimate responsibility`), to Art. 5(2)(d) (`overall responsibility for setting and approving the digital operational resilience strategy`), and to Art. 6(10) (`fully responsible for the verification of compliance`).
- **Cross-regulation consistency:** the 4-verb pattern is **unique to DORA** in the 5-regulation corpus. NIS 2 Art. 20 (governance) uses single-verb obligations (`approve`, `oversee`). CRA Art. 13(1) uses 3 verbs. GDPR Art. 24 (controller responsibility) uses `demonstrate compliance` (single verb). AI Act Art. 14 (human oversight) uses 4 verbs but in a different configuration. DORA's 4-verb pattern is the most elaborate.

#### 6.4 Art. 5(2)(a) and Art. 6(10) — non-delegable responsibility

Two clauses establish **non-delegable responsibility** of the management body / financial entity:

- **Art. 5(2)(a)** (CL5-3 Instance 1): `bear the ultimate responsibility for managing the financial entity's ICT risk`.
- **Art. 6(10)** (CL6-5 Instance 7): `the financial entity remains fully responsible for the verification of compliance with the ICT risk management requirements`.

The pattern is the **non-delegable accountability principle** — the management body cannot outsource the responsibility, even if it outsources the tasks. The principle is a **case-law concept** from banking supervision (Basel Committee) and EU financial-services law (CRD Art. 13, MiFID Art. 9 governance obligations).

- **Berry anchor:** §3.3.1 (`ultimate`, `fully`); §5.1 (`remains`).
- **Structural implication:** the non-delegable responsibility propagates to **out-sourcing** (Art. 28), **intra-group services** (Art. 28(4)), and **ICT third-party contracts** (Art. 30). A financial entity cannot escape Art. 5–6 obligations by contracting them out; the management body remains the accountable party.
- **Cross-regulation consistency:** the non-delegable responsibility is the **DORA-specific implementation** of a general EU financial-services law principle. NIS 2 has no equivalent (essential/important entities are not "governed" in the same way). CRA has no equivalent (manufacturers may delegate conformity assessment to notified bodies). GDPR Art. 24 (controller responsibility) has the same principle for data protection.

### 6. Cross-clause structural observations

## AI_Act

#### 2.9 AIA-C09 — Art. 12(1) Logging

**Source locus:** `High-risk AI systems shall technically allow for the automatic recording of events (logs) over the lifetime of the system`.

* **VAG — S2** — `over the lifetime` — undefined duration; cf. CRA support-period concept (Art. 13(8)).
* **POLY — S2** — `events (logs)` — distinct from security-event logs (cf. GDPR/DORA logging). AI-specific events (inference events, model-decision events, override events) are not enumerated.

**Berry anchor:** §5.1 (`lifetime`), §3.3.1 (`events`).

#### 2.10 AIA-C10 — Art. 12(2) Logging capabilities

**Source locus:** `In order to ensure a level of traceability of the functioning of a high-risk AI system that is appropriate to the intended purpose of the system, logging capabilities shall enable the recording of events relevant for: (a) … (b) … (c) …`.

* **VAG — S2** — `appropriate to the intended purpose`, `relevant for`.
* **POLY — S2** — `traceability` — ML ops traceability (training-run reproducibility) vs. runtime traceability (decision audit trail) vs. data-lineage traceability.
* **COORD — S2** — 3-element list (a)–(c) coordinated by AND.

**Berry anchor:** §5.1 (`appropriate`, `relevant`), §3.3.1 (`traceability`).

#### 2.11 AIA-C11 — Art. 12(4) (T5 reference — does not exist in OJ)

**T5 mapping mismatch** — T5 cites `Art. 12(4)` for `Retain logs for period appropriate to intended purpose; at least 6 months where applicable`. **The OJ Art. 12 has only paragraphs 1, 2, and 3.** Paragraph 4 does not exist. The substance of this clause (6-month minimum log retention) is in **Art. 19(1)** (`the logs shall be kept for a period appropriate to the intended purpose of the high-risk AI system, of at least six months`).

* The substance, when located at Art. 19(1) (cf. AIA-C19), carries:
* **VAG — S2** — `appropriate to the intended purpose`.
* **POLY — S2** — `under their control` (Art. 19(1)) — provider's control vs. deployer's control vs. joint control.

* Flagged for T5 authors: AIA-C11 and AIA-C19 appear to be duplicate mappings to two different articles, only one of which (Art. 19(1)) contains the substance.

**Berry anchor:** §5.1 (`appropriate`), §3.3.1 (`under their control`).

#### 2.19 AIA-C19 — Art. 19(1) Provider log retention

**Source locus:** `Providers of high-risk AI systems shall keep the logs referred to in Article 12(1), automatically generated by their high-risk AI systems, to the extent such logs are under their control. … the logs shall be kept for a period appropriate to the intended purpose of the high-risk AI system, of at least six months, unless provided otherwise in the applicable Union or national law`.

* **VAG — S2** — `appropriate to the intended purpose`.
* **POLY — S2** — `under their control` — provider's physical control vs. legal control vs. technical access.
* **SCOPE-Q — S2** — `unless provided otherwise in the applicable Union or national law, in particular in Union law on the protection of personal data` — the GDPR escape clause creates cross-regulation scope ambiguity.

* Hard numeric anchor (6 months) reduces temporal ambiguity relative to other notification clauses.

**Berry anchor:** §5.1 (`appropriate`), §3.3.1 (`under their control`), §5.2.1 (escape-clause scope).
