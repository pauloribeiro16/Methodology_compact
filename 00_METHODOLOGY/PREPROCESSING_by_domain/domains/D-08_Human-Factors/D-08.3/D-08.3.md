##### Pair: NIS2 ↔ DORA

**NIS2 article (verbatim, Art. 20(2) sentence 1):**
> "Member States shall ensure that members of the management bodies of essential and important entities are required to follow training, in order that they gain sufficient knowledge and skills to identify risks and assess cybersecurity risk-management practices and their impact on the services provided by the entity." — Art. 20(2) sentence 1 NIS 2 (per SR-NIS2-031)

**DORA article (verbatim, Art. 5(4)):**
> "Members of the management body shall actively keep up to date with sufficient knowledge and skills to enable them to understand and assess ICT risk and its impact on the operations of the financial entity, including by following specific training on a regular basis, commensurate to the ICT risk being managed." — Art. 5(4) DORA (per SR-DORA-024)

**Scope comparison:**
- NIS 2 scope: Member-State-imposed management-body training (the binding force is on the MS).
- DORA scope: direct entity-side management-body training obligation.
- Scope overlap: **Y (when the DORA financial entity is also an NSIE).**

**Trigger comparison:**
- NIS 2 trigger: Member-State obligation.
- DORA trigger: entity-side direct obligation.
- Trigger alignment: **PARTIAL** (both management-body training but different binding-force locus).

**Threshold/timeline comparison:**
- NIS 2: training mandatory under Art. 20(2) sentence 1 (Member-State-imposed); entity-side SR applies only where the MS has transposed.
- DORA: `actively keep up to date` + `regular basis` + `commensurate to the ICT risk`.
- Tension: **NONE** (both require sufficient knowledge and skills, but DORA is direct and ICT-risk-commensurate; NIS 2 is MS-imposed and literacy-prerequisite).

**Recipient comparison:**
- NIS 2: management body, competent authority.
- DORA: management body, NCA.

**Content template comparison:**
- NIS 2: not codified for content (entity-internal programme per SR-NIS2-031).
- DORA: not codified for content (entity-internal programme per SR-DORA-024).

**Classified relationship (from CRDA):** EQUAL
**Verified relationship (after OJ-text analysis):** **SAME — EQUAL**
**Reasoning:** Both regulations impose management-body training. DORA's OJ-literal anchor is direct (Art. 5(4) is the entity-side obligation); NIS 2's anchor is indirect (Art. 20(2) sentence 1 is the Member-State obligation, with the entity-side SR applying only where the Member State has transposed). For DORA financial entities, the DORA Art. 5(4) obligation is the dominant authority.

**Scope-disjoint test:** Does this pair actually overlap on a single event at a single party? **Y (when the DORA financial entity is also an NSIE applying management-body training)**. No reclassification required.

**Downstream implication (Phase 1C Doc 07):** Phase 2 obligation derivation must produce a DORA-dominant stack: DORA Art. 5(4) `actively keep up to date` + `commensurate to the ICT risk` discharges both DORA's dedicated management-body obligation and NIS 2's Member-State-imposed Art. 20(2) sentence 1 literacy prerequisite for DORA financial entities.

The `sufficient` S2 VAG is read at the role-relevant-competency end of the spectrum per the `commensurate to the ICT risk` qualifier (per SR-DORA-024 ambiguity_notes). The `regular basis` S2 VAG is read as risk-driven cadence per the literal `actively keep up to date` formulation (alternatives include scheduled or ongoing). The `commensurate to the ICT risk` S2 VAG — chosen reading is risk-proportional rather than risk-quantified. The `actively keep up to date` S2 POLY — alternatives include self-study, formal training, or both; chosen reading is the literal AND. PR.AT-03 anchors the training-and-knowledge component — `sufficient knowledge and skills` operationalised as documented role-and-responsibility literacy, evidence of `actively keep up to date`, and training records calibrated to the ICT risk being managed. GV.RR-01 layers the accountability-and-culture dimension — the management body's literacy tied to the personal-capacity prerequisite for the Art. 5(2) oversight-and-liability regime and to the broader fit-and-proper expectations under EBA Guidelines on Internal Governance, CRD Art. 91, and MiFID II Art. 4(1)(36)/(37). For a DORA financial entity that is also an NSIE with both NIS 2 and DORA applicability on the management-body-training substrate, the DORA Art. 5(4) `actively keep up to date` + `commensurate to the ICT risk` discharges both DORA's dedicated management-body obligation and NIS 2's Member-State-imposed Art. 20(2) sentence 1 literacy prerequisite, per CRDA-deep §8.3 NIS2↔DORA verified relationship (DORA-dominant for financial entities). For a DORA financial entity that is not an NSIE (DORA only), only the DORA sub-SO activates and the management-body training is discharged against the Art. 5(4) direct entity-side obligation with the NCA-supervised evidence trail. The **CRDA-unresolved interplay note** (AI Act Art. 4 may extend management-body competence to AI risk literacy for entities deploying high-risk AI in ICT-risk contexts) is preserved as a structural Layer 2 review item. (Copied from the HSO Considerations block of `### 8.3.2`; preserved here for self-contained traceability.)

#### 3.3 NIS2-CL03 — Art. 20(2) sentence 1 — Mandatory management-body training

**Source locus:**

> Member States shall ensure that the **<u>members of the management bodies</u>** of essential and important entities **are required to follow training**.

**Instance 1 — VAG — S3 — `are required to follow training`**

`required to follow training` — required by whom? Member States (the obligation target of the sentence is Member States) or the entity itself? Three materially distinct readings:

- **R1:** Member States must legislate a statutory training requirement for management-body members.
- **R2:** Member States must ensure the entity itself requires its management-body members to follow training.
- **R3:** Both (Member States must legislate AND ensure the entity requires it).

| # | Reading | Disambiguation source |
|---|---|---|
| R1 | Statutory training requirement | In-Directive: literal `Member States shall ensure` is directed to MS, not entities |
| R2 | Entity-internal requirement | In-Directive: literal `are required to follow` is directed to members |
| R3 | Both | In-Directive: standard MS-implementation pattern |

R1 and R2 yield materially different compliance obligations (R1 is a national-law requirement; R2 is an entity-internal governance requirement). S3.

*Berry anchor:* §5.1 (`required`).

**Instance 2 — VAG — S2 — `training`**

Polysemy inherited from the cybersecurity-vocabulary ecosystem. Three readings:

- **R1:** Formal certified curriculum (e.g. ISO/IEC 27001 lead-auditor, CISSP, CISM).
- **R2:** Awareness briefing (annual cybersecurity overview).
- **R3:** Tabletop exercise (incident simulation).

| # | Reading | Disambiguation source |
|---|---|---|
| R1 | Formal curriculum | None in OJ; ENISA training guidance |
| R2 | Awareness briefing | None in OJ; recital 50 (cyber hygiene) |
| R3 | Tabletop exercise | None in OJ |

Same polysemy as GDPR C27/C28 (training in data-protection context). The variant readings converge in practice (entities do all three) but the Directive does not specify which is required.

*Berry anchor:* §3.3.1 (`training`).

**Instance 3 — SCOPE-Q — S2 — `members of the management bodies`**

The Directive says `members` (universal). The reading:

- **R1:** Every individual member of every management body.
- **R2:** The management body as a collective (i.e. some members must be trained).
- **R3:** A designated cybersecurity-knowledgeable subset.

| # | Reading | Disambiguation source |
|---|---|---|
| R1 | Every member | In-Directive: literal `members` (plural) |
| R2 | At least one member (collective) | None in OJ |
| R3 | Designated subset | None in OJ |

R1 is the literal reading. R2 and R3 are operational concessions. S2 because the difference is between training-100% and training-a-subset.

### 4. Cross-clause structural observations (Art. 20)

1. **Art. 20(1) is the only direct personal-liability provision in NIS 2.** No other article in Chapter IV imposes liability on natural persons. The provision is short but materially consequential. National implementations have begun diverging (e.g. Belgium's transposition law, France's transposition law) on which liability types attach.
2. **Art. 20(2) creates a two-tier training obligation:** mandatory (for members) + encouraged (for employees). The asymmetry is intentional in the Directive; the operational impact is that entities may be judged on management-body training compliance but not on employee training.
3. **The management-body training obligation is the second cross-regulation `training` polysemy hotspot** (the first was GDPR Art. 39 DPO training; the third is CRA Annex I Part II §2(c) `security update procedures` which includes training). All three share the same POLY pattern.
4. **The `essential and important entities` classification propagates through every Chapter IV clause.** Resolving it is the prerequisite for any other finding.

## DORA

#### 6.8 Art. 5(4) — management body training (S3 from `sufficient`)

Art. 5(4) requires management body members to "actively keep up to date with **sufficient knowledge and skills**" and to follow "**specific training on a regular basis, commensurate to the ICT risk being managed**." The clause is **S3** for `sufficient` and `commensurate` (CL5-5 Instances 3-4) and is the **only Ch. II Sec. I clause** that addresses individual competence rather than institutional framework.

- **Berry anchor:** §5.1 (`sufficient`, `commensurate`); §3.3.1 (`knowledge`, `skills`).
- **Structural implication:** the training duty is **personal** (board members) not institutional. Failure to comply exposes individual board members to **personal liability** under national banking law (e.g. the German Kreditwesengesetz KWG §25c director liability; the French Code monétaire et financier). The clause interacts with **national fit-and-proper requirements** (EBA Guidelines on the assessment of the suitability of members of the management body).
- **Cross-regulation consistency:** the training duty is **DORA-specific**. NIS 2 Art. 20(2) requires the management body to "follow training" but uses a weaker formulation. CRA has no equivalent (manufacturers are not natural persons). GDPR Art. 38(2) (data protection officer) requires expert knowledge but at the function-holder level, not the management body.
