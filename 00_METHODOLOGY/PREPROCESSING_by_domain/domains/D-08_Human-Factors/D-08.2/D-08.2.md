##### Pair: GDPR ↔ NIS2

**GDPR article (verbatim, Art. 39(1)(b)):**
> "The data protection officer shall … monitor compliance with this Regulation, including the assignment of responsibilities, awareness-raising and training of staff involved in processing operations …" — Art. 39(1)(b) GDPR

**NIS2 article (verbatim, Art. 20(2) sentence 2):**
> "Member States shall encourage essential and important entities to offer similar training to their employees on a regular basis, in order that they gain sufficient knowledge and skills to identify risks and assess cybersecurity risk-management practices." — Art. 20(2) sentence 2 NIS 2

**Scope comparison:**
- GDPR scope: DPO-driven role-specific training for staff with data-subject-facing or access-management responsibilities.
- NIS 2 scope: Member-State-encouraged (not mandatory) employee training on risk identification and assessment.
- Scope overlap: **Conditional (Y when the controller-NSIE trains staff with both data-protection and risk-identification responsibilities).**

**Trigger comparison:**
- GDPR trigger: DPO-catalysed role-specific training.
- NIS 2 trigger: Member-State encouragement.
- Trigger alignment: **DIFFERENT** (DPO-direct vs. encouraged-indirect).

**Threshold/timeline comparison:**
- GDPR: no quantitative cadence.
- NIS 2: `on a regular basis` — entity-discretionary under Recital 56.
- Tension: **NONE**.

**Recipient comparison:**
- GDPR: data subject, DPA.
- NIS 2: management body, competent authority.

**Content template comparison:**
- GDPR: DPO-discretionary curriculum.
- NIS 2: not codified for content (entity-discretionary).

**Classified relationship (from CRDA):** COMPLEMENTARY
**Verified relationship (after OJ-text analysis):** **SAME — COMPLEMENTARY**
**Reasoning:** GDPR role-specific is DPO-catalysed and tied to processing role; NIS 2 employee training is encouraged and tied to risk-identification capability. Different content (data-protection vs. risk-identification) and different governance hooks (DPO vs. encouraged). The Art. 20(2) sentence 2 binding force is on the Member State rather than directly on the entity (Orchestrator-recommended treatment per SR-NIS2-030).

**Scope-disjoint test:** Does this pair actually overlap on a single event at a single party? **Conditional (Y when the controller-NSIE adopts the encouragement; N otherwise)**. No reclassification required.

**Downstream implication (Phase 1C Doc 07):** Phase 2 obligation derivation must produce a layered role-specific training stack: GDPR Art. 39(1)(b) DPO-catalysed (entity-side obligation) + NIS 2 Art. 20(2) sentence 2 encouraged (entity-side, conditional on MS encouragement adoption).

##### Pair: GDPR ↔ AI_Act

**AI_Act article (verbatim, Art. 14(4)(a)–(e) + Art. 26(2)):**

- Scope overlap: **Y (when the deployer-controller assigns human oversight for high-risk AI processing personal data).**

**Trigger comparison:**
- GDPR trigger: DPO-catalysed role-specific training.
- AI Act trigger: provider design (Art. 14) + deployer assignment (Art. 26).
- Trigger alignment: **DIFFERENT** (DPO-driven data-protection vs. AI-overseer competence).

**Threshold/timeline comparison:**
- GDPR: no quantitative cadence.
- AI Act: `as appropriate and proportionate` (qualitative) — 5 cumulative abilities must be enabled (Art. 14(4)).
- Tension: **NONE**.

**Recipient comparison:**
- GDPR: data subject, DPA.
- AI Act: deployer, provider, MSA.

**Content template comparison:**
- GDPR: DPO-discretionary curriculum.
- AI Act: 5-element cumulative abilities + `competence, training, authority, support` (Art. 26(2)).

**Classified relationship (from CRDA):** DIFFERENT-PERSPECTIVE
**Verified relationship (after OJ-text analysis):** **SAME — DIFFERENT-PERSPECTIVE**
**Reasoning:** GDPR role-specific targets processing staff with data-subject-facing responsibilities; AI Act targets human overseers of high-risk AI systems with the 5-element competence set. Different role, different content. The two can co-exist for a deployer that processes personal data via high-risk AI.

**Scope-disjoint test:** Does this pair actually overlap on a single event at a single party? **Conditional (Y when the same natural person is both a data-processing staff member and an AI human overseer)**. No reclassification required.

**Downstream implication (Phase 1C Doc 07):** Phase 2 obligation derivation must produce a layered stack: GDPR Art. 39(1)(b) processing-staff role-specific + AI Act Art. 14(4) + Art. 26(2) human-overseer competence. Where the same person holds both roles, the AI Act layer is the additional competence dimension.

**Objective.** High-risk AI systems are designed and developed in such a way, including with appropriate human-machine interface tools, that they can be effectively overseen by natural persons during the period in which they are in use (Art. 14(1) AI Act) — the provider-side design obligation for the human-oversight capability.

**Considerations.**
- The natural persons assigned to human oversight are enabled, as appropriate and proportionate, to discharge the 5 cumulative abilities of Art. 14(4): (a) properly understand the relevant capacities and limitations of the high-risk AI system and foreseeable misuse; (b) remain aware of automation bias; (c) correctly interpret the output; (d) decide not to use the high-risk AI system or to disregard, override or reverse its output; (e) intervene on the operation or interrupt the system through a stop button or similar procedure.
- The 5-element closed list is AND-coordinated (all five must be enabled cumulatively — partial compliance is non-compliance per SR-AIACT-013 ambiguity_notes).
- On the deployer-side assignment obligation, deployers of high-risk AI systems assign human oversight to natural persons who have the necessary competence, training and authority, as well as the necessary support (Art. 26(2) AI Act) — the 4-element AND `competence, training AND authority AND support` (all four required per SR-AIACT-020 ambiguity_notes).

##### Emergent tensions (5 regulations)
<!-- emergent: GDPR,NIS2,CRA,DORA,AI_Act -->

- **The five regulations impose role-specific competence on five different audiences**: GDPR DPO-driven processing staff; NIS 2 employees (encouraged); CRA integrators (B2B); DORA staff with `commensurate to the remit`; AI Act high-risk AI human overseers. The "commensurate to the remit" anchor in DORA is the broadest and absorbs the others in principle, but the OJ literal text in DORA is silent on AI competence.

The asymmetry — workforce training (NIS 2 / DORA / GDPR) vs. product-integrator training (CRA) — is unique to D-08.2. Phase 1 entities must distinguish these two training streams.

##### Downstream implication
Phase 1 entities must implement audience-routing role-specific training. A DORA financial entity deploying high-risk AI processing personal data must satisfy five role-specific obligations in parallel. The training-records documentation should be audience-tagged for audit traceability.

#### 3.6 NIS2-CL06 — Art. 20 chapeau — Implicit proportionality / scope hook

**Source locus (chapeau of Art. 20, paraphrased from sentence 1):**

### 4. Cross-clause structural observations (Art. 20)

1. **Art. 20(1) is the only direct personal-liability provision in NIS 2.** No other article in Chapter IV imposes liability on natural persons. The provision is short but materially consequential. National implementations have begun diverging (e.g. Belgium's transposition law, France's transposition law) on which liability types attach.
2. **Art. 20(2) creates a two-tier training obligation:** mandatory (for members) + encouraged (for employees). The asymmetry is intentional in the Directive; the operational impact is that entities may be judged on management-body training compliance but not on employee training.
3. **The management-body training obligation is the second cross-regulation `training` polysemy hotspot** (the first was GDPR Art. 39 DPO training; the third is CRA Annex I Part II §2(c) `security update procedures` which includes training). All three share the same POLY pattern.
4. **The `essential and important entities` classification propagates through every Chapter IV clause.** Resolving it is the prerequisite for any other finding.
