##### Emergent tensions (4 regulations)
<!-- emergent: GDPR,NIS2,CRA,DORA -->

- **Audience fragmentation**: GDPR (DPO-driven staff), NIS 2 (entity-wide hygiene), CRA (external users with product), DORA (compulsory staff modules) each target a different population and content scope. A DORA financial entity that processes personal data and ships CRA-regulated products must operate four parallel training programmes that do not cross-substitute. The AEGIS Cross-Cutting Layer 1 reconciliation may consolidate the GDPR / DORA staff modules but cannot substitute for CRA's user-facing instructions or NIS 2's hygiene baseline.
- **AI_Act is fully absent**: AI Act does not impose a general-security-awareness mandate — but AI Act Art. 4 (AI literacy) and SR-AIACT-012 (human-oversight training for high-risk AI) cover role-specific AI competence. The taxonomy marks these as D-08.2 (training & competence) not D-08.1 (general awareness), but the operational boundary between D-08.1 general and D-08.2 role-specific is undefined when AI literacy is at stake.

<!-- /emergent -->

##### SR cross-validation
The 4 applicable regulations map D-08.1 to the NIST CSF 2.0 Subcategories **PR.AT-01** (All users informed and trained on cybersecurity topics) as the convergent anchor. DORA additionally maps **PR.AT-02** (All members of the workforce understand their roles) via the `commensurate to the remit` clause; GDPR additionally maps **PR.AT-02** for the role-specific dimension. The CSF mapping reflects the staff-awareness substrate.

##### Downstream implication
Phase 1 entities must implement audience-fragmented training programmes. A consolidated training-management system can carry the four programmes (GDPR + NIS 2 + CRA user-instructions + DORA) with audience-routing logic. The training-records retention is per regulation (GDPR indefinite until processing ceases; DORA per Art. 5(2) accountability; CRA per Annex II §8 lifetime; NIS 2 per Recital 56 proportionality).

### 4. Cross-clause structural observations (Art. 20)

1. **Art. 20(1) is the only direct personal-liability provision in NIS 2.** No other article in Chapter IV imposes liability on natural persons. The provision is short but materially consequential. National implementations have begun diverging (e.g. Belgium's transposition law, France's transposition law) on which liability types attach.
2. **Art. 20(2) creates a two-tier training obligation:** mandatory (for members) + encouraged (for employees). The asymmetry is intentional in the Directive; the operational impact is that entities may be judged on management-body training compliance but not on employee training.
3. **The management-body training obligation is the second cross-regulation `training` polysemy hotspot** (the first was GDPR Art. 39 DPO training; the third is CRA Annex I Part II §2(c) `security update procedures` which includes training). All three share the same POLY pattern.
4. **The `essential and important entities` classification propagates through every Chapter IV clause.** Resolving it is the prerequisite for any other finding.

#### 6.2 Art. 28 umbrella — five governance blocks

Art. 28 is the **management-body-facing umbrella clause**. Its 10 paragraphs split into five operational blocks (mapped to CL28-1 through CL28-5):

| Block | Paragraphs | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Principles + strategy | chapeau + (1) + (2) | Integral component, full responsibility, proportionality, multi-vendor, mgmt body review |
| Register | (3) | Documentation + reporting to CA |
| Pre-contract | (4)(a)–(e) | 5 sub-steps before entering arrangement |
| Security + audit | (5) + (6) | Information security standards + audit + auditor competence |
| Termination + exit | (7) + (8) | 4 termination grounds + exit strategies for critical/important |
| RTS/ITS | (9) + (10) | ESAs standards (templates + policy content) |

Each block is a governance checkpoint. **Art. 28 is the longest single article in DORA** (10 paragraphs) and the most operationally consequential. Block (4) explicitly cross-references **Art. 29** for concentration risk (CL28-3 → CL29-1 / CL29-2).

#### 6.3 Art. 29 — Concentration risk as a regulatory hook

Art. 29 was **newly highlighted in v0.2** (T3 omitted it entirely). It serves three structural purposes:

1. **Cross-reference target from Art. 28(4)(c):** the pre-contract risk assessment must include concentration risk per Art. 29.
2. **Subcontracting + third-country + insolvency vectors:** four sub-paragraphs in Art. 29(2) cover the substantive risks that Art. 28 does not address.
3. **Bridge to Art. 30(3)(f) exit strategies:** concentration risk drives exit-strategy depth (CL30-15 Instance 2).

- **Cross-regulation parallel:** DORA-D29 `ICT concentration risk` is a **standalone definition** (Art. 3(29)), but Art. 29 is the only article operationalising it at entity level. **Art. 33(1)–(2)** is the **ESA-level** concentration-risk analogue (analysed in `05b`).
