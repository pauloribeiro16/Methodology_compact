## Security Rules (from 02_SecurityRules_NIST.md)

### SO-AIACT-001 (AI risk management lifecycle)

```yaml
- sr_id: SR-AIACT-007
  title: "Lifetime automatic logging of high-risk AI system events"
  source_clauses:
    - { clause_id: AIA-C09, article_ref: "Art. 12(1) — `High-risk AI systems shall technically allow for the automatic recording of events (logs) over the lifetime of the system`" }
    - { clause_id: AIA-C10, article_ref: "Art. 12(2) — `In order to ensure a level of traceability of the functioning of a high-risk AI system that is appropriate to the intended purpose of the system, logging capabilities shall enable the recording of events relevant for: (a) … (b) … (c) …`" }
  linked_objectives: [SO-AIACT-005]
  sub_domain: [D-10.2]
  nist_csf_mapping:
    - { id: PR.PS-04, title: "Log records are generated and made available for continuous monitoring and analysis" }
    - { id: DE.CM-09, title: "Computing hardware and software, runtime environments, and their data are monitored to find potentially adverse events" }
    - { id: DE.AE-03, title: "Event data are collected and correlated from multiple sources and sensors" }
  applies_to_role: [PROVIDER]
  obligation_type: [CONTINUOUS, ONE_TIME]
  regulatory_rationale: |
    Article 12(1) of the AI Act requires that high-risk AI systems
    technically allow for the automatic recording of events (logs)
    over the lifetime of the system. Article 12(2) operationalises
    this requirement by mandating that logging capabilities ensure a
    level of traceability of the functioning of the high-risk AI
    system that is appropriate to the intended purpose of the
    system, by enabling the recording of events relevant for
    ensuring traceability of the functioning of the system,
    monitoring its operation, and facilitating post-market
    monitoring under Article 72. Together the two paragraphs form
    the log-generation and log-capability substrate for the AI-Act
    post-market-monitoring apparatus. The lifetime-scope qualifier
    ties logging into the broader lifecycle obligations under
    Articles 9 and 17 and mirrors the log-retention duty that DORA
    Article 12 places on financial entities and that NIS 2 Article
    21 places on essential and important entities.
  security_rationale: |
    The obligation in Art. 12(1)–(2) is operationalised in NIST
    CSF 2.0 through **PR.PS-04 (Log records are generated and
    made available for continuous monitoring and analysis)**,
    **DE.CM-09 (Computing hardware and software, runtime
    environments, and their data are monitored to find potentially
    adverse events)**, and **DE.AE-03 (Event data are collected
    and correlated from multiple sources and sensors)**. PR.PS-04
    anchors the lifetime automatic-event-recording substrate — the
    provider must technically enable log generation across the full
    AI-system lifecycle, including AI-specific events (inference
    decisions, model decisions, override events) implied by the
    `functioning of a high-risk AI system` opening of Art. 12(2)
    beyond conventional security logs. DE.CM-09 covers the
    runtime-monitoring dimension of the three-element purpose list
    (traceability, monitoring, post-market monitoring), supporting
    live operational monitoring alongside post-incident
    reconstruction. DE.AE-03 supplies the cross-source event
    correlation that converts the raw log stream into an
    audit-grade narrative, including cross-regulation flows under
    NIS 2 Art. 23 and DORA Art. 19. A provider documenting
    PR.PS-04 log generation, DE.CM-09 continuous-monitoring
    coverage and DE.AE-03 multi-source correlation demonstrates
    ex post that the Art. 12(1)–(2) lifetime logging duty —
    including AI-specific events beyond conventional security logs
    — is reconstructable for supervisory review.
  ambiguity_notes: |
    The phrase `over the lifetime` is vague and undefined in the
    AI Act; the chosen reading aligns lifetime with the
    product-lifecycle definition in Article 3(1)(c), by analogy
    with the CRA support-period concept in CRA Article 13(8). The
    phrase `events (logs)` is poly-semous, since AI-specific events
    such as inference events, model-decision events and override
    events are not enumerated in the Act; the chosen reading is
    broad, covering AI-specific events per the `functioning of a
    high-risk AI system` opening of Article 12(2). As an
    alternative, providers could read `events` as security-event
    logs familiar from DORA and NIS 2, which is narrower and
    excludes AI-specific inference-decision events. The phrase
    `appropriate to the intended purpose` in Article 12(2) is
    vague, and the term `traceability` is poly-semous between
    ML-ops training-run reproducibility, runtime decision
    audit-trail traceability and data-lineage traceability; the
    chosen reading is cumulative, covering all three senses. The
    three-element list in Article 12(2) (a)–(c) is AND-coordinated.
    Remain open: whether `over the lifetime` aligns lifetime
    duration with the deployer's deployment window or with the
    manufacturer's broader product-lifecycle window extending into
    post-decommissioning support obligations.
```

### SO-AIACT-001 (AI risk management lifecycle)

```yaml
- sr_id: SR-AIACT-008
  title: "Six-month minimum log retention aligned with intended purpose"
  source_clauses:
    - { clause_id: AIA-C11, article_ref: "Art. 12(4) per T5 — **T5 mapping error**: Art. 12 has only paragraphs 1, 2, 3 in the OJ; the substance is in Art. 19(1)" }
    - { clause_id: AIA-C19, article_ref: "Art. 19(1) — `the logs shall be kept for a period appropriate to the intended purpose of the high-risk AI system, of at least six months, unless provided otherwise in the applicable Union or national law`" }
  linked_objectives: [SO-AIACT-006]
  sub_domain: [D-10.2, D-05.2]
  nist_csf_mapping:
    - { id: PR.PS-04, title: "Log records are generated and made available for continuous monitoring and analysis" }
    - { id: GV.PO-02, title: "Cybersecurity processes and procedures for implementing the cybersecurity policy are established, communicated, and enforced" }
  applies_to_role: [PROVIDER]
  obligation_type: [CONTINUOUS]
  regulatory_rationale: |
    Article 19(1) of the AI Act requires providers of high-risk AI
    systems to keep the logs referred to in Article 12(1) —
    automatically generated by their high-risk AI systems and to
    the extent that such logs are under their control — for a
    period appropriate to the intended purpose of the high-risk AI
    system, of at least six months, unless provided otherwise in
    the applicable Union or national law. The clause anchors the
    AI-Act log-retention duty to the upstream log-generation duty
    in Article 12(1) and to the provider-side control allocation.
    The six-month retention floor provides the only hard numeric
    anchor for retention duration across the AI Act; the soft
    anchor `appropriate to the intended purpose` permits upward
    adjustment for higher-risk or longer-lived systems. The escape
    clause defers to GDPR Article 5(1)(e), NIS 2 incident-log
    retention and DORA Article 12 where those apply, leaving
    financial-entity providers with concurrent log-retention
    obligations.
  security_rationale: |
    The obligation in Art. 19(1) is operationalised in NIST CSF
    2.0 through **PR.PS-04 (Log records are generated and made
    available for continuous monitoring and analysis)** and
    **GV.PO-02 (Cybersecurity processes and procedures for
    implementing the cybersecurity policy are established,
    communicated, and enforced)**. PR.PS-04 anchors the
    log-generation substrate that the six-month retention floor
    requires — without continuous log generation, the retention
    duty is vacuous and the post-market monitoring and incident
    investigation flows have no reconstructable trail. GV.PO-02
    supplies the process-and-procedure substrate that the upward
    adjustment clause (`appropriate to the intended purpose`)
    requires, ensuring that retention duration beyond the
    six-month floor is justified by the system's risk class and
    documented in the provider's cybersecurity policies and
    procedures. Together, the two sub-categories cover the
    retention floor (PR.PS-04) and the retention-justification
    methodology (GV.PO-02) that Art. 19(1) requires. A provider
    evidencing PR.PS-04 log-generation continuity and GV.PO-02
    retention-procedure documentation demonstrates ex post that
    the Art. 19(1) six-month floor is operationalised, with
    documented justification for any upward adjustment under the
    `appropriate to the intended purpose` clause and the
    cross-regulation interplay with GDPR Art. 5(1)(e), NIS 2
    incident-log retention and DORA Art. 12.
  ambiguity_notes: |
    The T5 mapping flag is preserved: AIA-C11 references Article
    12(4), which does not exist in the OJ text of the AI Act;
    Article 12 has only paragraphs 1, 2 and 3. The substantive rule
    on six-month log retention is correctly located at Article
    19(1), which is also the locus of AIA-C19. The chosen reading
    preserves the OJ-literal locus at Article 19(1) and surfaces
    the T5 mapping error per Berry fidelity; the recommendation to
    T5 authors is to remove AIA-C11 as a duplicate or remap it to
    Article 19(1). The phrase `under their control` is poly-semous,
    with possible readings as physical control, legal control or
    technical access; the chosen reading is cumulative, covering
    all three senses. As an alternative, providers could read
    `under their control` narrowly as physical control only. The
    `appropriate to the intended purpose` qualifier is vague, and
    the escape-clause scope ambiguity creates cross-regulation
    friction with GDPR, NIS 2 and DORA log-retention. Remain open:
    whether the T5 mapping reconciliation will consolidate AIA-C11
    into AIA-C19 or remove AIA-C11 entirely.
```

### SO-AIACT-001 (AI risk management lifecycle)

```yaml
- sr_id: SR-AIACT-009
  title: "Provider-side log-retention responsibility across hosting models"
  source_clauses:
    - { clause_id: AIA-C09, article_ref: "Art. 12(1) — log-generation capability" }
    - { clause_id: AIA-C19, article_ref: "Art. 19(1) — `Providers of high-risk AI systems shall keep the logs referred to in Article 12(1)`" }
  linked_objectives: [SO-AIACT-006]
  sub_domain: [D-10.2]
  nist_csf_mapping:
    - { id: PR.PS-04, title: "Log records are generated and made available for continuous monitoring and analysis" }
    - { id: RC.RP-03, title: "The integrity of backups and other restoration assets is verified before using them for restoration" }
  applies_to_role: [PROVIDER]
  obligation_type: [CONTINUOUS]
  regulatory_rationale: |
    Article 19(1) of the AI Act, sentence 1, requires providers of
    high-risk AI systems to keep the logs referred to in Article
    12(1) — the automatically generated event logs — to the extent
    that such logs are under their control. The clause allocates
    responsibility for log retention to the provider, distinct from
    the upstream log-generation duty in Article 12(1) and the
    downstream supervisory-access duty in Article 21(1). The `under
    their control` qualifier is the responsibility-allocation
    hinge of the article, since the physical location of logs may
    vary between provider-hosted infrastructure, deployer-hosted
    infrastructure and third-party cloud infrastructure. This SR
    focuses on the responsibility-allocation aspect of Article
    19(1); the temporal-duration aspect (six-month minimum) is
    covered in SR-AIACT-008.
  security_rationale: |
    The obligation in Art. 19(1) sentence 1 is operationalised in
    NIST CSF 2.0 through **PR.PS-04 (Log records are generated and
    made available for continuous monitoring and analysis)** and
    **RC.RP-03 (The integrity of backups and other restoration
    assets is verified before using them for restoration)**.
    PR.PS-04 anchors the log-availability substrate that the
    responsibility-allocation hinge requires — the provider must
    keep the Art. 12(1) logs available under their control
    regardless of where the underlying compute substrate is
    physically hosted. RC.RP-03 supplies the integrity-verification
    handle that supports the retention duty when logs are
    reconstructed from backups or other restoration assets,
    ensuring that the supervisory-access flow does not encounter
    corrupted records at audit time. Together, the two
    sub-categories cover log availability (PR.PS-04) and
    log-integrity through restoration (RC.RP-03) that the
    responsibility-allocation hinge operationalises. A provider
    evidencing PR.PS-04 log availability and RC.RP-03
    restoration-integrity verification demonstrates ex post that
    the Art. 19(1) sentence-1 responsibility-allocation duty is
    discharged across hybrid and multi-cloud deployments,
    including the cross-cloud architectures common in
    DORA-regulated financial-entity deployments where logs may
    span provider-, deployer- and third-party-hosted infrastructure.
  ambiguity_notes: |
    The phrase `to the extent such logs are under their control`
    is poly-semous and carries medium severity; the chosen reading
    is cumulative, covering provider's physical control
    (provider-hosted logs), provider's legal control
    (deployer-hosted logs but under provider's contractual
    obligation to retain) and provider's technical access
    (deployer-hosted logs with provider's read-access). As an
    alternative reading, providers could limit the obligation to
    logs under their physical control only, since the cross-reference
    to Article 12(1) `automatic recording of events` suggests a
    broad reading but does not exclude a narrow reading. The
    upstream AIA-C11 / Article 12(4) T5 mapping flag from
    SR-AIACT-008 also propagates here as a structural reference.
    Remain open: whether the `under their control` allocation
    will be enforced as a substantive obligation mandating
    contractual flow-down to deployers and third-party hosts, or
    as a procedural obligation mandating only documentation of the
    responsibility allocation.
```
