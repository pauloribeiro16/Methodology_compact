  cascading-impact minimisation: the product is designed to protect

#### Cross-recipient map

D-04.2 itself does **not** generate recipient asymmetry — the
containment-and-mitigation process is **inward-looking** (the entity
takes measures to contain and mitigate the incident's impact on its

**Objective.** Security incidents affecting the entity's systems, products, services, components and data are contained and mitigated through appropriate measures that address the incident's impact and reduce the probability of further damage.

**Considerations.**
- All four participating regulations (GDPR, NIS 2, CRA, DORA) impose a containment-and-mitigation duty but each targets a distinct containment layer and anchors to a distinct threshold: GDPR's post-breach TOMs (technical and organisational measures) including unintelligibility rendering (Art. 33(3)(d) + Art. 34(3)(a)/(b) unintelligibility carve-out; the unintelligibility rendering is GDPR-unique and acts as a substitute for Art. 34 data-subject notification when applied); NIS 2's entity-side incident-handling `contain` phase within the 6-action lifecycle (Art. 21(2)(b) + Art. 6(8) six-action definition with `contain` as one of the six actions); CRA's product-side availability preservation + cascading-impact minimisation + exploitation mitigation (Annex I Part I (2)(h) DoS resilience + (2)(i) cross-product impact minimisation + (2)(k) exploitation mitigation; the three-clause constellation is CRA-unique and operates at the product-design level); DORA's financial-entity-side BC arrangements with response and recovery plans + backup policies + restoration procedures (Art. 11(1)/(2)/(3) + Art. 12(1); the BC-arrangements architecture is DORA-unique and the strictest containment-architecture floor in the AEGIS corpus).
- The high-level SO surfaces the common containment-and-mitigation duty (contain the incident's impact, mitigate further damage, take measures to address the breach / incident, route to the downstream recovery / notification pipeline); the per-regulation perspective (containment layer, measure type, threshold qualifier, evidence template) is preserved in the four sub-SOs.
- A single organisation across all four regulators must operate a multi-layer containment architecture with explicit layer hand-offs: personal-data runbook feeds entity-level response plan at the controller/NSIE boundary; entity-level response plan feeds product resilience architecture via deployment (CRA products instrument their resilience & exploitation mitigation to support the deployer's containment); product resilience feeds back into DORA BC arrangements via integration. For parties that touch only one regulatory scope, only the triggered sub-SO applies.
- The high-level SO does NOT collapse the four containment layers into a single measure type — the layers contain different objects (personal-data breach impact vs all-hazards entity event impact vs product availability vs ICT infrastructure) and produce different measure types (TOMs incl. unintelligibility render vs lifecycle-phase actions vs product-design choices vs BC arrangements).
- The high-level SO is honesty-preserving about scope-disjoint cases: for a 5-FTE SaaS startup with GDPR + CRA applicability but no NIS 2 / DORA applicability, only the GDPR and CRA sub-SOs activate; the high-level SO engages on the same party if both apply, with the boundary between personal-data runbook (GDPR) and product-resilience architecture (CRA) documented as the controlled hand-off.
- DORA's BC arrangements with response-and-recovery plans (Art. 11(2)/(3)) are the strictest containment-architecture floor — they require dedicated, appropriate and documented arrangements, plans, procedures and mechanisms (the 4-way AND), plus independent internal audit for non-microenterprises (Art. 11(3) — the audit requirement is DORA-unique) — and exceed the NIS 2 `contain` requirement for financial-sector entities also within retained NIS 2 scope.

#### Scope-overlap principle (operational). The high-level SO
(`SO-D-04.2.HL`) engages on a given party **if and only if ≥2 applicable
regulations' containment-layer scope-determiners overlap on the same

documented in the entity response plan (NIS 2); product-availability

The downstream notification cascade (Art. 19(1) competent-authority + Art. 19(3) client notification) is part of 4.3, not 4.2. **Clause: GDPR-RT16** | type: data subject right (object) | obligatedParty: CONTROLLER | obligationType: PER-OBJECTION

**Instance 1 — VAG / S3 / `compelling legitimate grounds`**

#### Verbatim (with highlighting)

> **Article 35**
> **Data protection impact assessment**
>

> (a) a **systematic description** of the envisaged processing operations and the purposes of the processing, including, where applicable, the legitimate interest pursued by the controller;

> (b) an assessment of the **necessity and proportionality** of the processing operations in relation to the purposes;

> (c) an assessment of the risks to the rights and freedoms of data subjects referred to in paragraph 1;

### 4. Cross-clause structural observations (Art. 13)

1. **Art. 13(2) is the operational core of the manufacturer's risk-assessment duty.** The 6-phase lifecycle propagation (CL16) is the **longest single coordination pattern in CRA** — 6 phases coordinated by AND. Berry §5.5 parallelism admits partial-compliance readings.

6. **6 S3 clauses (CL15, CL16, CL18, CL19, CL23a, CL27, CL28, CL33, CL42):** risk assessment, 6-phase propagation, documentation, intended purpose + foreseeability, due diligence, support-period factors, 10-year updates, single point of contact. **No S1 instances.**

### 5. T3-vs-text gaps

This section catalogues **inconsistencies between the v0.1 T3 mapping** and the OJ text. Per pilot convention, T3-vs-text gaps are flagged separately from linguistic ambiguities of the regulation (they are inconsistencies of the mapping layer, not of the text).

#### 6.3 Art. 6 chapeau — the framework's three-attribute triad

Art. 6(1) opens with `sound, comprehensive and well-documented` (three attributes, AND). This is the **framework-level analog** of:

- **Art. 24(1) `sound and comprehensive`** (testing programme, two attributes; CL24-1)
- **CRA Annex I Part I §1 chapeau** `products with digital elements shall be designed, developed and produced …` (manufacturing lifecycle, three verbs; CRA-CL14)
- **NIS 2 Art. 21(1) `appropriate and proportionate technical, operational and organisational measures`** (measures chapeau)

The 3-attribute triad (`sound, comprehensive and well-documented`) is **the most demanding framework chapeau in the 5-regulation corpus** — it requires three distinct qualities (effectiveness, completeness, traceability). CRA Annex I Part I §1 has three verbs (design, develop, produce) but they describe **processes** not qualities. DORA's triad describes **qualities of an artifact** (the framework itself), which is a different kind of obligation.

- **Berry anchor:** §5.1 (`sound`, `comprehensive`, `well-documented`); §5.4.7 (AND); §5.5 (parallelism).
- **Structural implication:** the framework's three attributes set the bar for every Ch. II–V Sec. I clause. Any clause that fails one of the three (e.g. a framework that is `comprehensive and well-documented` but not `sound`) would be non-compliant.
- **Cross-regulation consistency:** the triad is the most explicit framework-quality specification in the corpus. NIS 2 Art. 21(1) uses `appropriate and proportionate` (2 qualifiers); CRA Annex I Part I uses process verbs; GDPR Art. 24 uses `appropriate` (1 qualifier). DORA's 3-attribute triad is the most demanding.

#### 6.6 Art. 6(8) — the 8-point resilience strategy

Art. 6(8) enumerates **8 strategy elements** (open list: `include … by`). The 8 elements cover the strategy's full lifecycle: (a) business alignment, (b) risk tolerance, (c) KPIs/KRMs, (d) reference architecture, (e) detection/prevention, (f) current state evidence, (g) testing, (h) communication.

- **Berry anchor:** §3.3.1 (`include … by`); §5.4.7 (8-way enumeration).
- **Structural implication:** the 8-point strategy is the **operational mirror** of the framework's three-attribute triad (CL6-1 Instance 1). Where the triad describes the framework's qualities, the 8 elements describe its **contents**. A framework that is `sound, comprehensive and well-documented` and contains all 8 elements satisfies the chapeau.
- **Cross-regulation consistency:** the open-list pattern is the **regulatory-drafting convention** shared with CRA Annex I Part I (multiple open enumerations) and NIS 2 Art. 21(2) (10 sub-points). DORA's 8 elements are **more strategic** (KPIs, risk tolerance, communication) than CRA's (technical requirements) and **more operational** than NIS 2's (governance policies).

#### 6.5 Art. 21 — single EU Hub feasibility report (RTS-style structural delegation)

Art. 21 mandates a **feasibility report** by the ESAs Joint Committee (in consultation with ECB and ENISA) on the potential centralisation of incident reporting through a **single EU Hub**. The report must comprise **7 elements** (a)–(g).

This is **not an obligation on financial entities** — it is a meta-procedural mandate on the ESAs. But its outcome (whether or not a single EU Hub is established) could materially alter the entity-side reporting architecture. As of Q1 2025 (post the 17 January 2025 deadline), the ESAs Joint Committee report was published; the legislative follow-up is uncertain.

- **Berry anchor:** §3.3.1 (`single EU Hub`); §5.1 (`feasibility`); §3.3.1 (`interoperability`).
- **Structural implication:** the **single EU Hub** is structurally analogous to the **NIS 2 single point of contact (SPOC)** (NIS 2 Art. 12) and the **CER Directive critical-entity framework** but at a higher integration level.
- **Layer 1 catalogue only.** Layer 2 must surface the architectural implications (sensitive-information concentration, jurisdictional sovereignty, operational resilience of the hub).

#### 6.9 Art. 19(5) — outsourcing + responsibility clause

Art. 19(5) permits the financial entity to **outsource** the reporting obligations under Art. 19 to a third-party service provider (under Union and national sectoral law), **with the financial entity remaining fully responsible**. This mirrors DORA Art. 26(3) `retain at all times full responsibility for ensuring compliance with this Regulation` (for TLPT) and DORA Art. 22(1) `Without prejudice to the supervisory feedback received, financial entities shall remain fully responsible`.

- **Berry anchor:** §3.3.1 (`in accordance with`); §5.1 (`fully responsible`).
- **Structural implication:** the **responsibility non-delegation principle** is consistent across Ch. III (Art. 19(5)), Ch. IV (Art. 26(3)), and Art. 22(1). A financial entity cannot outsource its reporting compliance by outsourcing the reporting process.
- **Cross-regulation consistency:** same pattern as GDPR Art. 28(4) (processor obligations do not discharge controller obligations) and NIS 2 Art. 21(1) (management-body responsibility).
