**Participants (from CRDA):** GDPR, CRA

##### Pair: GDPR ↔ CRA

**GDPR article (verbatim):**
> Art. 25(2) obliges the controller to implement appropriate technical and organisational measures for ensuring that, by default, only personal data which are necessary for each specific purpose of the processing are processed. That obligation applies to the amount of personal data collected, the extent of their processing, the period of their storage and their accessibility. In particular, such measures shall ensure that by default personal data are not made accessible without the individual's intervention to an indefinite number of natural persons.

**CRA article (verbatim):**
> Annex I Part I (2)(b) requires products to be made available on the market with a secure-by-default configuration — including the possibility to reset the product to its original state — unless otherwise agreed between manufacturer and business user in relation to a tailor-made product with digital elements. Art. 6(a) proviso anchors the configuration to intended purpose and reasonably foreseeable use.

**Objective.** Personal-data processing systems implement appropriate
technical and organisational measures ensuring that, by default, only
personal data which are necessary for each specific purpose of the
processing are processed — across the four AND-coordinated dimensions
of amount of data collected, extent of their processing, period of their
storage, and their accessibility — with the additional constraint that
personal data are not made accessible without the individual's
intervention to an indefinite number of natural persons (Art. 25(2) +
Art. 25(1) privacy-by-design cross-anchor + Art. 5(1)(c) data
minimisation underlying principle).

**Considerations.**
- The four-dimensional AND is preserved from `SR-GDPR-013` `ambiguity_notes` (all four dimensions must individually be minimised and AND-coordinated per EDPB Guidelines 04/2019 on Article 25; an alternative reading allowing some dimensions to be relaxed if others are tightened would weaken the default and is rejected; the AND reading is the chosen one).
- On `without the individual's intervention`, the chosen reading treats the intervention requirement as the opt-in gate: the controller must not bypass the data subject's affirmative action to expose data.
- The data-centric by-default is distinct from the CRA product-centric secure-by-default — the data-processing layer is system/configuration-of-data-side, and the four-dimension AND discipline operates at that layer only.
- The CRA tailor-made carve-out does NOT propagate into GDPR scope: a CRA tailor-made product configuration does not exempt the controller from GDPR Art. 25(2).
- For an organisation across both regulators, the GDPR leg documents the data-side leg of the two-layer default posture rather than imposing a separate framework; the boundary hand-off to CRA product-secure-by-default is documented as the controlled layer interface at which the data-centric four-dimension AND ends.
- The downstream notification cascade (Art. 33 personal-data-breach notification) is part of D-04.3, not D-03.4.

#### D-03.4 Secure System Defaults

**Participants (from CRDA):** GDPR, CRA

##### Pair: GDPR ↔ CRA

**GDPR article (verbatim):**
> Art. 25(2) obliges the controller to implement appropriate technical and organisational measures for ensuring that, by default, only personal data which are necessary for each specific purpose of the processing are processed. That obligation applies to the amount of personal data collected, the extent of their processing, the period of their storage and their accessibility. In particular, such measures shall ensure that by default personal data are not made accessible without the individual's intervention to an indefinite number of natural persons.

**CRA article (verbatim):**
> Annex I Part I (2)(b) requires products to be made available on the market with a secure-by-default configuration — including the possibility to reset the product to its original state — unless otherwise agreed between manufacturer and business user in relation to a tailor-made product with digital elements. Art. 6(a) proviso anchors the configuration to intended purpose and reasonably foreseeable use.

**Scope comparison:**
- GDPR scope: data-protection by default across amount, extent, period and accessibility of personal data.
- CRA scope: secure-by-default configuration of the product, including reset capability, with the tailor-made carve-out.
- Scope overlap: Conditional when a CRA product processes personal data.

**Trigger comparison:**
- GDPR trigger: continuous data-protection-by-default obligation.
- CRA trigger: continuous product secure-by-default obligation at market placement.
- Trigger alignment: PARTIAL.

**Threshold/timeline comparison:**
- GDPR: appropriate by-default measures across four dimensions.
- CRA: secure by default with reset capability; tailor-made carve-out for business users.
- Tension: NONE.

**Recipient comparison:**
- GDPR: data subjects and supervisory authority.
- CRA: product users and market surveillance.

**Content template comparison:**
- GDPR: by-default settings across four Art. 25(2) dimensions.
- CRA: secure-by-default configuration and reset procedure.

**Classified relationship (from CRDA):** DIFFERENT-PERSPECTIVE

**Verified relationship (after OJ-text analysis):** SAME — DIFFERENT-PERSPECTIVE

**Reasoning (1-2 paragraphs):** GDPR is data-centric by default; CRA is product-centric by default. The OJ text confirms that they target different objects at different layers. There is no contradiction, even in overlap cases, because the GDPR Art. 25(2) four-dimension test operates on the data and the CRA Annex I (2)(b) test operates on the product configuration.

**Scope-disjoint test:** Does this pair actually overlap on a single event at a single party? Conditional
- If N or Conditional: the CRDA "contradictory" classification is **scope-incorrect**; it should be "scope-disjoint" in baseline. This pair was not classified contradictory.

**Downstream implication (1-2 sentences):** Doc 07 should treat data-by-default and product-secure-by-default as two layers of the same default posture, with documentation showing how each dimension is satisfied.

### 4. Cross-clause structural observations (Art. 25)

1. **Art. 25 is encouragement-only.** No binding entity obligation. The article's operational impact is on the entity's evidence base: standards cited in Art. 25(1) and ENISA guidelines produced under Art. 25(2) are non-mandatory but provide a soft path to Art. 21 compliance.

2. **The technology-neutrality clause is the most distinctive single phrase.** `without imposing or discriminating in favour of the use of a particular type of technology` is the only explicit technology-neutrality mandate in Chapter IV. It is a structural commitment to standards-based (not technology-specific) regulation.

3. **ENISA's `shall draw up advice and guidelines` is one of the few mandatory provisions on ENISA in Chapter IV.** Most ENISA references in Chapter IV are `may` (Art. 21(5), Art. 22(1), Art. 23(11), Art. 24(3)). Art. 25(2) is `shall draw up` — a positive obligation.

4. **Cross-regulation parallel.** Art. 25 mirrors the standardisation provisions in CRA Art. 27 (presumption of conformity via harmonised standards) and AI Act Art. 40 (conformity assessment via harmonised standards). The `European and international standards and technical specifications` triplet is the standard cross-regulation formulation.

#### 6.3 Art. 6 chapeau — the framework's three-attribute triad

Art. 6(1) opens with `sound, comprehensive and well-documented` (three attributes, AND). This is the **framework-level analog** of:

- **Art. 24(1) `sound and comprehensive`** (testing programme, two attributes; CL24-1)
- **CRA Annex I Part I §1 chapeau** `products with digital elements shall be designed, developed and produced …` (manufacturing lifecycle, three verbs; CRA-CL14)
- **NIS 2 Art. 21(1) `appropriate and proportionate technical, operational and organisational measures`** (measures chapeau)

The 3-attribute triad (`sound, comprehensive and well-documented`) is **the most demanding framework chapeau in the 5-regulation corpus** — it requires three distinct qualities (effectiveness, completeness, traceability). CRA Annex I Part I §1 has three verbs (design, develop, produce) but they describe **processes** not qualities. DORA's triad describes **qualities of an artifact** (the framework itself), which is a different kind of obligation.

- **Berry anchor:** §5.1 (`sound`, `comprehensive`, `well-documented`); §5.4.7 (AND); §5.5 (parallelism).
- **Structural implication:** the framework's three attributes set the bar for every Ch. II–V Sec. I clause. Any clause that fails one of the three (e.g. a framework that is `comprehensive and well-documented` but not `sound`) would be non-compliant.
- **Cross-regulation consistency:** the triad is the most explicit framework-quality specification in the corpus. NIS 2 Art. 21(1) uses `appropriate and proportionate` (2 qualifiers); CRA Annex I Part I uses process verbs; GDPR Art. 24 uses `appropriate` (1 qualifier). DORA's 3-attribute triad is the most demanding.

#### 6.1 Art. 25(1) — 12-modality open list (POLY/S3)

Art. 25(1) enumerates 12 specific test modalities under the `such as …` open-list signal. The structural pattern is **identical to CRA Annex I Part I §2(d)** (`authentication, identity or access management systems` under `including but not limited to`) and to the CRA Annex I enumeration generally.

- **Berry anchor:** §5.1 `such as` open-list marker; same pattern as CRA-C05 (Annex I Part I §2(d)).
- **Structural implication:** the 12 modalities are illustrative, not exhaustive. Financial entities may add (e.g. red team, social engineering, fuzz testing) but should not omit any of the 12 without justification.
- **Cross-regulation consistency:** CRA, NIS 2 (Art. 21(2)(e) `secure system development and maintenance` includes similar open enumerations), and DORA all converge on the open-list pattern. This is a **regulatory-drafting convention**, not an artefact of any single regulation.

#### 6.5 Art. 25(2) — Sectoral carve-out for CSD/CCP

Art. 25(2) imposes a **pre-deployment vulnerability assessment obligation on two specific entity types** (CSDs and CCPs). This is a sectoral carve-out unique in Ch. IV — no other clause names specific entity types.

- **Berry anchor:** §3.3.1 (`central securities depositories`, `central counterparties`).
- **Structural implication:** CSDs and CCPs are **financial market infrastructures (FMIs)** with systemic importance. The pre-deployment assessment obligation reflects the FMI-specific risk profile (a deployment-induced vulnerability could cascade to the entire clearing or settlement system).
- **Cross-regulation consistency:** CRA does not impose pre-deployment obligations. NIS 2 Art. 21(2)(f) requires `policies and procedures for cryptography` but not pre-deployment assessments. **DORA Art. 25(2) is a sectoral specificity.**
