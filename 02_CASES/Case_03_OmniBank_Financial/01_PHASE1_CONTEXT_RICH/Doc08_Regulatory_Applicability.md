### 3.1 GDPR (General Data Protection Regulation)

| Criterion | Company Context Value | Threshold | Met? |
|-----------|----------------------|-----------|------|
| processes_personal_data | TRUE (customer PII, financial transactions, credit scores) | ANY | YES |
| special_category_data | FALSE (financial data not Art. 9) | ANY | N/A |
| EU data subjects | TRUE (customers across EU) | ANY | YES |
| large_scale_processing | TRUE (millions of customers, billions of transactions) | ANY | YES |

**Applicability Result:** ✅ **APPLICABLE**

**Rationale:** OmniBank processes personal data (customer PII, financial transactions, credit scores, behavioral data) as a controller for customer relationships. Large-scale processing triggers mandatory DPIA (Art. 35). Financial data is not special category under Art. 9, but requires enhanced safeguards due to sensitivity.

**Key Clauses in Scope:** GDPR-C01 through GDPR-C28 (all 28 clauses applicable)

**Obligated Party:** CONTROLLER (primary for customer data)

**Multi-Actor Note:** GDPR Art. 32 (security of processing) applies **directly to both controllers AND processors** ("the controller and the processor shall implement..."). While OmniBank is primarily a controller for customer data, any third-party processors handling OmniBank customer data have direct Art. 32 obligations (encryption, access control, breach notification) — not merely contractual obligations.

**Nuance — DPO Rationale (Art. 37):** OmniBank does NOT process special category data (Art. 9) — financial data is not a special category. However, OmniBank's core activities involve **large-scale systematic monitoring** of customer financial behaviour (transaction monitoring, credit scoring, fraud detection), which triggers the DPO requirement under **Art. 37(1)(b)**. The DPO obligation is mandatory but the rationale is systematic monitoring, not Art. 9 processing.

**Nuance — Processor Breach Notification (Art. 33(2)):** OmniBank's cloud providers and payment processors (acting as processors for OmniBank's customer data) must notify OmniBank (controller) of personal data breaches "without undue delay" — a distinct obligation from OmniBank's 72h notification to the supervisory authority. The processor→controller clock has no fixed numeric deadline and should be contractually defined (e.g., within 4-8 hours).

### 3.2 CRA (Cyber Resilience Act)

| Criterion | Company Context Value | Threshold | Met? |
|-----------|----------------------|-----------|------|
| places_digital_products_eu | TRUE (mobile banking app + web platform in EU) | TRUE | YES |
| digital_element | TRUE (software with AI elements) | YES | YES |
| manufacturer_status | TRUE (OmniBank develops mobile apps) | YES | YES |
| product_class | STANDARD (not critical like border control) | Standard | YES |

**Applicability Result:** ✅ **APPLICABLE (Standard Class)**

**Rationale:** OmniBank mobile banking app and web platform are digital products placed on the EU market. As manufacturer, OmniBank must comply with CRA Essential Requirements (Annex I). Standard Class allows self-declaration of conformity (not notified body like SecureBorder).

**Key Clauses in Scope:** CRA-C01 through CRA-C26 (all 26 clauses applicable)

**Obligated Party:** MANUFACTURER (self-declaration of conformity)

**CRA Product Classes (context):** The CRA defines four conformity assessment regimes:
1. **Default products** → self-assessment (Module A, internal control)
2. **Important products Class I** (Annex III Class I, e.g., VPNs, password managers, consumer firewalls) → self-assessment *only if* harmonised standards exist, else third-party (Module B+C or H)
3. **Important products Class II** (Annex III Class II, e.g., enterprise firewalls, IDS, secure elements) → mandatory third-party conformity assessment
4. **Critical products** (Annex IV, e.g., smart cards with secure elements, smart meter gateways) → mandatory European cybersecurity certification (level "substantial")

OmniBank's mobile banking app qualifies as a **Standard/Default** product under CRA (not listed in Annex III or IV). Self-declaration of conformity suffices.

**CRA Annex I: Two Parts with Different Natures:**
- **Part I** — Essential requirements *relating to the properties of products* (encryption, integrity, access control). These are "what the product must be" — **ProductProperty** obligations.
- **Part II** — Essential requirements *relating to the vulnerability handling processes* (SBOM, vulnerability management, security updates, CVD policy). These are "what the manufacturer must do throughout the product lifecycle" — **OrganisationalProcess** obligations.

**Two Reporting Flows (both under Art. 14):**
The CRA has **two separate reporting obligations**, both within **Art. 14** (not Art. 15 — see caveat below):

| Flow | Article | Trigger | Early Warning | Notification | Final Report |
|------|---------|---------|---------------|--------------|--------------|
| **Actively Exploited Vulnerabilities** | Art. 14(1-2) | Manufacturer becomes aware of active exploitation | 24h to CSIRT/ENISA | 72h to CSIRT/ENISA | **14 days after corrective measure is available** |
| **Severe Incidents affecting product security** | Art. 14(3-5) | Severe incident impacting product security | 24h to CSIRT/ENISA | 72h to CSIRT/ENISA | **1 month after incident notification** |

**Caveat — Art. 15 is Voluntary:** Art. 15 of the CRA is titled "Voluntary reporting" and allows manufacturers to voluntarily report vulnerabilities, incidents, and near misses. The **mandatory** severe incident reporting is under **Art. 14(3)**, not Art. 15. This is a common misattribution.

The final report deadline differs fundamentally: 14 days post-fix for vulnerabilities vs 1 month post-notification for incidents. The 14-day clock starts when a corrective measure is *available*, not when the vulnerability was discovered.

**Quantitative Thresholds:**
- **Support Period (Art. 13(8)):** Minimum **5 years** from product placement (or expected use time if less). For OmniBank's mobile banking app, this means security patches for at least 5 years per app version.
- **Security Update Retention (Art. 13(9)):** Security updates must remain available for **10 years** or the support period, whichever is longer. Old app versions' patches must be accessible for a decade.
- **Technical Documentation Retention (Art. 13(13)):** Technical documentation must be kept for **10 years** after the product has been placed on the market.

### 3.3 NIS 2 (Network and Information Systems Directive 2)

| Criterion | Company Context Value | Threshold | Met? |
|-----------|----------------------|-----------|------|
| nis2_sector | Financial Services (Annex I) | Essential | YES |
| size (employees) | 5000+ | ≥50 (medium) | YES |
| size (revenue) | >€1.5B | ≥€10M | YES |
| essential_entity_status | TRUE (systemic importance for financial stability) | TRUE | YES |

**Applicability Result:** ✅ **APPLICABLE (Essential Entity)**

**Rationale:** OmniBank qualifies as a large enterprise (5000+ employees, >€1.5B revenue) operating in the financial sector (NIS 2 Annex I). As a credit institution, it is automatically classified as an Essential Entity per NIS 2 Art. 3(1). Management liability applies for non-compliance.

**Key Clauses in Scope:** NIS2-C01 through NIS2-C29 (all 29 clauses applicable)

**Obligated Party:** ESSENTIAL_ENTITY (24h incident notification, management liability)

**Nuance — Delegated Granularity (Implementing Regulation 2024/2690):**
The NIS 2 Art. 21(5) delegates technical detail to Commission implementing acts. The **Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/2690** specifies granular technical and methodological requirements for specific sectors (DNS, cloud, data centres, CDNs, MSSPs, etc.). **However, credit institutions are NOT in the enumerated list** — DORA is the *lex specialis* for ICT risk management in the financial sector. OmniBank's NIS 2 obligations as an Essential Entity are governed by the directive text and German transposition law, not by IR 2024/2690. For ICT-specific technical requirements, DORA's RTS framework is the relevant delegated legislation.

**Nuance — Trust Service Provider Derogation (Art. 23(4)):**
For trust service providers in OmniBank's supply chain, the incident notification deadline is **24h** (not the standard 72h). Relevant if OmniBank uses qualified trust services for digital signatures, timestamps, or certificates.

**Nuance — Ongoing Incident Provision (Art. 23(4)(e)):**
The 1-month final report deadline is not absolute — if the incident is still ongoing, a progress report is due at 1 month, with the final report due within one month of incident handling completion.

**Directive vs. Regulation Note:** NIS 2 is a **Directive**, not a Regulation. Germany (OmniBank's jurisdiction) transposed NIS 2 via the NIS-2-Umsetzungsgesetz. The exact sanctions regime and enforcement thresholds may differ from other Member States. This creates jurisdiction-specific variation not captured by treating NIS 2 as a uniform EU instrument.

### 3.4 DORA (Digital Operational Resilience Act)

| Criterion | Company Context Value | Threshold | Met? |
|-----------|----------------------|-----------|------|
| dora_financial_entity | TRUE (credit institution per Art. 2) | TRUE | YES |
| financial_sector_classification | Credit institution (banking) | YES | YES |
| ict_third_party_provider | FALSE (not primarily ICT provider) | YES/NO | N/A |

**Applicability Result:** ✅ **APPLICABLE (Financial Entity)**

**Rationale:** OmniBank is a credit institution per DORA Art. 2(1)(a) — supervised by ECB/BaFin. All DORA requirements apply: ICT risk management (Art. 5-16), incident reporting (Art. 17-19), digital operational resilience testing (Art. 24-27), third-party risk (Art. 28-31). 100% Weight 3 obligations (unconditional).

**Key Clauses in Scope:** DORA-C01 through DORA-C38 (all 38 clauses applicable)

**Obligated Party:** FINANCIAL_ENTITY (100% unconditional obligations)

**Nuance — Deadlines Come from RTS, Not DORA Text:**
DORA Art. 19(4) does **not** specify numeric deadlines in the regulation text itself. It states that entities submit notifications "within the time limits to be laid down in accordance with Article 20." The concrete deadlines are specified in the **Regulatory Technical Standard (RTS)** — JC 2024-33, adopted by the Commission in October 2024 via Delegated Regulation (EU) 2025/301.

The actual DORA incident reporting deadlines (via RTS) are:

| Phase | Deadline | Reference |
|-------|----------|-----------|
| Initial notification | 4 hours after classification as major, **never more than 24 hours** after discovery | RTS Art. 6(1)(a) |
| Intermediate report | 72 hours after initial notification | RTS Art. 6(1)(b) |
| Final report | 1 month (30 days) after classification as major | RTS Art. 6(1)(c) |

This distinction is important: DORA establishes the obligation; the RTS establishes the specific timelines. A reviewer noting "DORA doesn't say 4 hours anywhere" would be technically correct — the 4-hour deadline is in the RTS, which is legally binding as a delegated act.

**Nuance — Weekend Clause (RTS):**
The RTS allows deadlines falling on weekends to be deferred to the next business day at noon — **except** for credit institutions, CCPs, trading venues, and essential entities with >250 employees / >€50M turnover. As a credit institution with 5000+ employees, OmniBank is **not eligible** for the weekend deferral — deadlines apply 24/7.

**Nuance — CTPP Direct Obligations (Arts. 28-44):**
DORA creates a **direct oversight framework** over Critical ICT Third-Party Providers (CTPPs) designated by the Lead Overseers (ESAs). CTPPs (e.g., major managed hosting providers when designated) face:
- Submission of information on legal structure, risk management, incident response
- Binding recommendations from Lead Overseers
- Obligation to remediate identified risks
- Potential fines up to €5M or 1% of global turnover for non-compliance with recommendations

These are **direct regulatory obligations** on non-financial entities, not merely contractual obligations via DPA. This means OmniBank's ICT suppliers, if designated as CTPPs, face independent regulatory scrutiny — which indirectly strengthens OmniBank's supply chain compliance posture.

### 3.5 AI Act (Artificial Intelligence Regulation)

| Criterion | Company Context Value | Threshold | Met? |
|-----------|----------------------|-----------|------|
| aiact_high_risk_system | TRUE (credit scoring per Annex III) | Annex III | YES |
| ai_system_provider | TRUE (OmniBank develops AI models) | YES | YES |
| high_risk_use_case | TRUE (credit scoring — Annex III §5) | Annex III listing | YES |
| biometric_categorization | FALSE (not biometric identification) | Annex III §1 | N/A |

**Applicability Result:** ✅ **APPLICABLE (High-Risk AI System)**

**Rationale:** OmniScore AI Platform is a high-risk AI system per AI Act Annex III §5 (credit scoring). As provider, OmniBank must comply with all High-Risk AI requirements: risk management (Art. 9), data governance (Art. 10), technical documentation (Art. 11-13), human oversight (Art. 14), accuracy/robustness/cybersecurity (Art. 15), conformity assessment (Art. 43), post-market monitoring (Art. 72).

**Key Clauses in Scope:** AI-C01 through AI-C29 (all 29 clauses applicable)

**Obligated Party:** PROVIDER (High-Risk AI System per Annex III)

**AI Act Risk Architecture (5 layers):** The AI Act has a **hierarchy of risk** with distinct obligations per layer:
1. **Prohibited AI** (Art. 5) — Certain practices banned entirely (social scoring, cognitive manipulation). Zero clauses applicable — absolute prohibition.
2. **High-risk AI** (Art. 6 + Annex III) — The bulk of technical obligations (Arts. 9-15: risk management, data governance, transparency, human oversight, accuracy/robustness/cybersecurity) apply *only* to this category. OmniScore AI falls here (credit scoring — Annex III §5).
3. **GPAI models** (Art. 51+) — Transparency and documentation obligations for general-purpose AI models.
4. **GPAI models with systemic risk** (Art. 55) — Additional layer: adversarial testing, incident reporting, cybersecurity protection for models with "high-impact capabilities" (> 10²⁵ FLOPs training).
5. **Limited-risk AI** (Art. 50) — Minimal transparency obligations (chatbots, deepfakes).

OmniBank's OmniScore AI is classified as **High-Risk AI** (layer 2). It does not appear to be a GPAI model (layers 3-4) since it is a domain-specific credit scoring model, not a general-purpose foundation model.

**Three Tiers of Serious Incident Reporting (Art. 73):**
The AI Act has three different deadlines for serious incident reporting depending on severity:

| Scenario | Deadline | Base Legal |
|----------|----------|------------|
| Default (serious incident) | No later than **15 days** after becoming aware | Art. 73(2) |
| Widespread infringement OR serious incident Art. 3(49)(b) | Immediately, no later than **2 days** | Art. 73(3) |
| Death of a person | No later than **10 days** after establishing causal link | Art. 73(4) |

All three tiers apply to OmniBank as a provider of a high-risk AI system. The 10-day tier (death) is the most stringent in terms of causal investigation requirements.

**Downstream Provider Obligations (Art. 25):** If a distributor, importer, or deployer substantially modifies OmniScore AI (e.g., rebranding it as their own credit scoring product, or modifying the model for a different risk assessment purpose), that entity becomes the "provider" with full Art. 16 obligations. If a partner bank white-labels OmniScore and deploys it under their own brand, the partner becomes the provider. OmniBank should include written agreements (Art. 25(4)) with any downstream entities specifying information, capabilities, and technical access needed for compliance. This is particularly relevant if OmniBank plans to license OmniScore to other financial institutions.
